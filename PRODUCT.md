

# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Maritime operators, crewing managers, compliance officers, and business owners at shipping companies. Tech vendors and business clients who evaluate solutions based on outcomes — they want to see what changed before-and-after, not just what was built. They visit to understand whether AhumAI can fix their specific operational problems.

Secondary: investors, potential hires, and industry peers who judge credibility through specificity, not polish.

## Product Purpose

This landing page is the first handshake. It must communicate that AhumAI has actually built things — not concepts, not demos — and that the team understands maritime operations from the inside. The primary job is booking 30-minute discovery calls. The secondary job is earning trust fast enough that the visitor wants to book one.

## Brand Personality

Honest, Capable, Relentless.

- **Honest**: Direct language. No hype. Clear about what is live vs. what is being built. States limitations openly because that is what earns trust.
- **Capable**: Confidence comes from specificity — naming real numbers (500+ certificates/day), real products (NavCert, Sorch), real outcomes (a role that no longer exists). Never generic claims.
- **Relentless**: Belief-driven, not process-driven. Three uncompromising beliefs show on every page. Small team, fast shipping, no excuses.

Tone: maritime-native. Uses industry language without explaining it (PSC inspections, STCW, crewing). Assumes the reader knows the domain. Does not sound like a generic AI company that happened to pick maritime.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: hero mockup + "Features" + "Pricing" + gradient CTAs. No context, no proof.
- Big-corporate "capabilities deck" aesthetics: slideware pretending to be a website. Stock photography of ships.
- AI-startup clichés: neural network visuals, glowing brain icons, purple-to-cyan gradients everywhere, "revolutionizing X with AI."
- Companies pretending to be larger than they are: "global leader," "enterprise-grade," phantom office addresses.
- Glassmorphism as default, gradient text, side-stripe borders, identical card grids (absolute bans).
- Novu-style tech-marketing: sparkles, purple glows, "ship faster" aesthetic. AhumAI is maritime — not another dev-tool company cosplaying maritime colors.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** Never claim capability. Show a before-and-after. Show a number. Show a specific workflow that changed. If there is no proof for a statement, don't make the statement.

2. **Maritime-native, not tech-generic.** The site should feel like it was built by people who know what a PSC inspection is. Domain language without explanation. Visual references that resonate with shipping operations, not Silicon Valley.

3. **Small and fast beats large and slow.** The site itself should feel fast, lean, intentional. No bloat. No unnecessary sections. Every element earns its place — the same way the company builds products.

4. **Honesty first.** Clear about what is live, what is in development, what is coming. No deceptive status indicators. The differentiation is trust — trust is built by telling visitors when something isn't done yet.

5. **Outcome over artifact.** The story is not "we built NavCert." The story is "a role that no longer exists because of NavCert." Every section should lead with the before-and-after transformation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum. High contrast for maritime users who may access from bright deck environments or dim bridge displays.
- Graceful performance on lower-bandwidth connections (vessels, remote ports). No assumption of fast fiber.
- Reduced motion support for vestibular accessibility.
- Keyboard-navigable, screen-reader friendly.
- Text alternatives for all images and meaningful icons.
